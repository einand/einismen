        <section class="section">
          <h1>The core of Einismen</h1>
          <p class="lead">The pillars describe how Einismen holds together worldview, character, freedom, integrity and change in one direction.</p>
        </section>

        <figure class="image-break">
          <img src="../../media/en/pillars-hero.svg" alt="">
          <figcaption>Five pillars carrying direction and steadiness.</figcaption>
        </figure>

        <section class="section split">
          <div class="span-4"><h3><a href="chaos">Accept chaos</a></h3><p>The world is not simple. The first step is to see that clearly.</p></div>
          <div class="span-4"><h3><a href="inner-strength">Build inner strength</a></h3><p>Courage, discipline and reflection are needed as conditions change.</p></div>
          <div class="span-4"><h3><a href="freedom">Live freely with responsibility</a></h3><p>You choose, but you also carry the consequences.</p></div>
          <div class="span-6"><h3><a href="weight-of-words">Let words carry weight</a></h3><p>Words without action become empty. Integrity matters.</p></div>
          <div class="span-6"><h3><a href="change">Improve further</a></h3><p>Perfection is not the goal. The next clear improvement matters more.</p></div>
        </section>

        <section class="section prose">
          <h2>Five supporting principles</h2>
          <p>The five pillars begin from one shared insight: the world is complex and a person must orient themselves. The first pillar accepts uncertainty. The second builds the inner strength needed to stand firmly. The third shows how freedom and responsibility belong together. The fourth insists that words and action must be kept together. The fifth keeps improvement alive.</p>
          <p>Each pillar depends on the previous one. Without accepting chaos, inner strength becomes difficult to justify. Without inner strength, freedom becomes risky. Without responsibility, words turn empty. Without integrity, change becomes restlessness rather than development.</p>
        </section>

        <section class="section prose">
          <h2>Function before dogma</h2>
          <p>Through all of Einismen runs a recurring principle of method: what works should be understood and refined, and what no longer works should be reconsidered. This attitude carries all five pillars and keeps them alive rather than static.</p>
        </section>
