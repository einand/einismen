          <h1>Criticism and objections</h1>
          <p class="lead">Einismen can be perceived as hard, strict or excessively rational. These are reasonable objections and they need to be met openly.</p>

          <figure class="image-break">
            <img src="../../../media/en/questions-hero.svg" alt="">
            <figcaption>Criticism that tests rather than rejects.</figcaption>
          </figure>

          <p>Common criticism is that the focus on function can sound cold or instrumental, and that the philosophy's demands for integrity and responsibility can become heavy for people who already live under strong pressure. Another objection is that chaos as a starting point can be perceived as dark or destabilizing.</p>
          <p>Einismen's answer lies in the fact that the philosophy does not try to make the human being mechanical, but more reality-near. Clarity is not the same as coldness. Function is not the same as indifference. The philosophy rather wants to protect the human being from illusions that weaken them over time.</p>
          <p>The weakness is still there: Einismen risks becoming too strict if it loses its human tone. That is why its own principles also need to be tested again and again.</p>
