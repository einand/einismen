        <section class="section prose">
          <h2>Basics</h2>
          <p>This block gathers the most common first questions about what Einismen is, how it relates to truth and why chaos is so central.</p>
          <ul class="qa-list">
            <li><strong>Is Einismen a religion?</strong>No. It is primarily described as a philosophy and a way of thinking.</li>
            <li><strong>What is the core?</strong>See clearly. Choose for yourself. Take responsibility. Improve further.</li>
            <li><strong>Is it cold?</strong>It tries to be sober and useful. That is not the same as being emotionless.</li>
            <li><strong>Why does the word function return so often?</strong>Because something should be kept when it actually serves a purpose.</li>
            <li><strong>How does it view change?</strong>Change is natural. What matters is direction and understanding.</li>
            <li><strong>How does it view emotions?</strong>Emotions matter, but they should not govern decisions on their own.</li>
            <li><strong>What does Einismen say about truth?</strong>That truth should be sought through clear observation, reasoning and testing, not through comfortable illusions.</li>
            <li><strong>What does chaos mean in Einismen?</strong>Chaos means that the world is uncertain, shifting and not made to fit our wishes.</li>
            <li><strong>Is Einismen pessimistic?</strong>No. It is realistic in its starting point but constructive in its answer.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section class="section prose">
          <h2>The human being and life</h2>
          <p>Here lie questions about responsibility, strength, virtues, mortality and what Einismen is actually trying to shape in the human being.</p>
          <ul class="qa-list">
            <li><strong>Why is responsibility so central?</strong>Because freedom without responsibility becomes empty, and responsibility without freedom becomes coercion.</li>
            <li><strong>What is meant by inner strength?</strong>The ability to think clearly, stand firm, reflect and continue even when the situation is difficult.</li>
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view virtues?</strong>Virtues are practical tools, not ornaments. They should help the human being live steadily and effectively.</li>
            <li><strong>Is Einismen individualistic?</strong>Yes, but not in a careless way. It emphasizes self-chosen direction but also responsibility toward others.</li>
            <li><strong>What does the weight of words mean?</strong>That words should be carried by action, otherwise they lose their weight.</li>
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view death?</strong>Death is not hidden. Life's limitation makes choices sharper and time more meaningful.</li>
            <li><strong>What does Einismen say about perfection?</strong>Perfection is less important than steady refinement and directed improvement.</li>
            <li><strong>What is the goal of the philosophy?</strong>Not to create comfort, but to create clearer lives, stronger character and better systems.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section class="section prose">
          <h2>Method and practice</h2>
          <p>This block focuses on how the philosophy is used: in work, development, improvement and the way ideas are tested.</p>
          <ul class="qa-list">
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view work?</strong>Work should be refined, made clearer and become better over time instead of stagnating.</li>
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view leadership?</strong>Leadership should be sober, independent, responsible and open to better evidence.</li>
            <li><strong>What is dialectical improvement?</strong>Letting ideas meet resistance, understanding their weaknesses and then building a stronger synthesis.</li>
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view development?</strong>Development is a duty. What can be improved should be improved.</li>
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view stagnation?</strong>Stagnation is seen as dangerous, because it causes the human being or the system to stop learning.</li>
            <li><strong>How do you know whether something is compatible with Einismen?</strong>If it survives testing, is carried by integrity and leads to real improvement, it lies close to the philosophy's core.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section class="section prose">
          <h2>Ethics and objections</h2>
          <p>Here questions are gathered about morality, conflict and how to judge whether something lies near or far from the core of Einismen.</p>
          <ul class="qa-list">
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view morality?</strong>Morality is treated as something that must be thought through in real situations, not merely inherited as phrases.</li>
            <li><strong>How does Einismen view conflicts?</strong>Necessary conflicts should not be avoided, but conflict in itself has no intrinsic value.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>
